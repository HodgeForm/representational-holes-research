# What Happens When AI Cannot Ask the Right Question?

*Draft — not for publication until the underlying experiment and proof artifacts have been checked.*

Most AI research asks how to improve a system's answers. But sometimes a system fails because its measurements cannot express a distinction needed for the task.

This is a **representational hole**: distinct target-relevant situations are mapped to the same observations by an explicitly restricted language.

In earlier experiments with elementary cellular automata, a translation-invariant measurement grammar reportedly could not distinguish Rules 3 and 17. A symmetry argument offers an explanation for that limitation within the specified grammar. A later parity-sensitive measurement reportedly distinguished the rules on 217 of 256 evaluated states.

The distinction between a search failure and a language-wide impossibility matters. Testing many unsuccessful candidates is not itself a proof that no candidate in the language could work. Such a conclusion requires a precisely stated grammar and a valid proof or exhaustive certificate.

The proposed research cycle is: identify observational equivalence, establish a bounded limitation, introduce a discriminating measurement, and test it prospectively. Whether this approach generalizes beyond these controlled examples remains an open research question.

This is not a claim of AGI or a universal scientific discovery law. It is a testable method for understanding why a specified representation fails, and for evaluating targeted repairs.
